**[Host Tone: Grave, urgent]**
We talk about Artificial Intelligence as if it’s magic. We talk about the "cloud" as if it’s a weightless, invisible realm where data just floats. But the cloud is just someone else's physical computer. And right now, the massive AI hardware boom is violently colliding with the physical limits of the Earth.

**[VISUAL CUE: Transition to Infographic Background - AI factory spewing data streams into heavy water/carbon footprints]**

The scale of the infrastructure build-out happening right now is difficult to comprehend. This week, Nvidia and the LG Group announced a partnership to build massive "AI Factories." We aren't building server rooms anymore; we are building industrial-scale manufacturing plants dedicated entirely to processing matrix multiplications. 

The hardware requirements to train and run the next generation of frontier models are staggering. But while investors cheer the silicon advancements, the environmental reality is catching up with us.

**[VISUAL CUE: Display Key Highlight Slide]**
*(Text: The UN warns of massive, unsustainable energy, carbon, and water footprints.)*

**[Host Tone: Emphatic, factual]**
This week, the United Nations Institute for Water, Environment and Health issued a severe, uncompromising report detailing the environmental cost of AI. And the numbers are terrifying. 

We are talking about an energy footprint that rivals entire developed nations. We are talking about carbon emissions from data centers that are actively derailing global climate goals. And perhaps most critically, we are talking about water. The millions of gallons of fresh water required every single day just to cool the server racks training these models. 

We are currently operating under the delusion that we can brute-force our way to Artificial General Intelligence by simply building bigger buildings and plugging in more GPUs. But the UN report makes it explicitly clear: this trajectory is physically unsustainable. You cannot achieve infinite digital scaling on a planet with finite physical resources.

The bottleneck to AGI is no longer software. It isn't even silicon. It is the power grid. It is the local water reservoir.
